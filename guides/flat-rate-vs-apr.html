Flat Rate vs APR — Which Personal Loan Is Really Cheaper?

A 4% flat-rate loan sounds cheaper than a 7% APR loan. Often it isn't. Here's why, with worked numbers, and how to compare any two offers in a minute.

Updated: 2026-11-10

Two ways to quote interest

  • Flat rate: interest is charged on the original amount for the whole term, even though you repay part of it every month.
  • APR / reducing balance: interest is charged only on what you still owe, so it shrinks every month.

Worked example: 50,000 over 4 years

OfferMonthlyTotal interestReal APR
4% flat1,208.338,000.00≈ 7.47%
7% APR1,197.317,470.997.00%

The “4%” offer is actually more expensive than the 7% one. As a rule of thumb, a flat rate costs a little under twice its headline number in APR terms.

The formulas

  • Flat: monthly = (P + P × rate × years) ÷ months.
  • Amortized: monthly = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n), with r = annual rate ÷ 12 and n = months.

Don't forget fees

Upfront admin fees raise the real cost because you receive less money but repay the same instalments. The loan calculator includes fees in the APR and shows a yearly schedule, so you can compare offers on the same basis.

Frequently asked questions

Is a flat rate always worse?

For the same headline number, yes — compare offers by APR instead.

What is a good APR?

It depends on your country and credit; compare at least two or three offers by APR and total cost.

Does Islamic murabaha financing use flat rates?

Profit is usually quoted as a fixed (flat) rate on the original amount; the calculator shows its APR equivalent for comparison.

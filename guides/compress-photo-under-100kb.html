How to Compress a Photo to Under 100 KB for Online Forms

Job portals, exam registrations and visa forms often reject photos over 100 KB (or 50 KB, or 20 KB). Here's how to hit the limit while keeping the photo sharp — and why “99 KB” files sometimes still fail.

Updated: 2026-11-10

File size vs dimensions

File size is in KB/MB; dimensions are in pixels (e.g. 600×800). A typical phone photo is 3–5 MB at about 4000×3000 px — 30–50× over a 100 KB limit.

Three steps

  1. Open the compress-to-KB tool and choose the limit (100 KB, 50 KB, 20 KB…).
  2. If the form also requires dimensions (e.g. 200×230 px), enter them as max width/height.
  3. Drop the photo and download. The tool shows the final size, dimensions and quality.

It searches for the highest quality that fits and only shrinks dimensions when quality alone isn't enough. In our test a 3.6 MB phone photo became 97.8 KB at 1210×907 px and 65% quality — perfectly clear for an application photo.

The “99 KB but rejected” trap

Some systems count 1 KB as 1,000 bytes, others as 1,024. A 101,000-byte file shows as “99 KB” in Windows but fails a 100,000-byte check. The tool targets 100,000 bytes exactly, so it passes either way.

Typical limits

  • Signatures: 20 KB or less.
  • Passport-size photos: 50 KB to 100 KB.
  • Visa and ID uploads: often up to 200 KB.

Avoid

  • Screenshots of the photo (changes dimensions and adds blur).
  • Sending it through messaging apps to “shrink” it — the result is unpredictable.
  • HEIC files from iPhones — convert to JPG first.

Frequently asked questions

How do I check a photo's size?

Windows: right-click → Properties. Mac: Get Info. The tool also shows before/after sizes.

Will the photo look blurry?

It keeps the highest quality that fits and shrinks dimensions only as much as needed.

Is my photo uploaded?

No, compression happens in your browser.

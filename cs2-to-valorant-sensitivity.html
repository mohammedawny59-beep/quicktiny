CS2 to Valorant Sensitivity Converter

Switching from Counter-Strike 2 to Valorant? Divide your CS2 sensitivity by 3.1818 at the same DPI. The converter below does it for you.

Example

Quick answers

Why does it feel different even with the same cm/360?

Field of view differs between games, so small movements feel different at first.

How the conversion works

Every game turns the camera a fixed number of degrees per mouse count at sensitivity 1 (its "yaw"). Matching your aim means matching the physical distance you move the mouse for a full 360° turn — your cm/360. The converter computes your cm/360 in the first game and finds the sensitivity that gives the same distance in the second.

  • cm/360 = 360 ÷ (DPI × sensitivity × yaw) × 2.54
  • eDPI = DPI × sensitivity — handy for comparing players in the same game.
  • Changing DPI? Enter the new DPI and you keep the same feel.

Notes per game

  • Converts hip-fire (look) sensitivity. Scoped/ADS multipliers differ by game and are not included.
  • Rainbow Six Siege assumes the default MouseSensitivityMultiplierUnit (0.02) in GameSettings.ini.
  • Fortnite uses the X-axis sensitivity percentage shown in its settings.
  • Turn off "enhance pointer precision" (mouse acceleration) in Windows for consistent aim.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert Valorant sensitivity to CS2?

Multiply your Valorant sensitivity by about 3.18 (0.07 ÷ 0.022). For example, Valorant 0.35 at 800 DPI is CS2 1.114 — the converter does this for you.

What's a good cm/360?

Most tactical-shooter pros (CS2, Valorant) sit around 35–55 cm/360. Faster games like Apex or Overwatch tend toward 20–35 cm. Lower cm/360 means faster turning.

Why does it feel slightly different after converting?

The 360° distance matches exactly, but field of view differs between games, so small aim adjustments can feel different. Give it a few sessions.
